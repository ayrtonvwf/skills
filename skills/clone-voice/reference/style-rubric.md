# Style rubric

The fixed set of dimensions to analyze a corpus against. Apply **every** dimension so
profiles are comparable across people. For each one, record concrete observations and
quote **real examples drawn from the supplied corpus** - never invent examples. If a
dimension has no signal in the corpus, say so explicitly rather than guessing.

## Dimensions

1. **Punctuation habits** - use of commas, periods, ellipses (`...`), exclamation and
   question marks, dashes, parentheses. Does the person end messages without a period?
   Stack punctuation (`!!`, `?!`)? Overuse or avoid commas?
2. **Capitalization** - sentence case, all-lowercase, ALL CAPS for emphasis, capitalized
   Proper Nouns or not, capitalization of "i".
3. **Typos and grammar quirks** - habitual misspellings, missing apostrophes (`dont`,
   `im`), homophone swaps (`your`/`you're`), agreement slips, run-ons or fragments.
   Capture these as **reproducible traits**, not errors to correct - fidelity is the goal.
4. **Emoji use and context** - which emoji, how often, and in what situations (sign-off,
   reaction, softening a request, humor). Note if emoji are absent.
5. **Slack-style `:emoji-name:` use and context** - literal shortcodes like `:tada:` or
   `:this:`, custom reactions, and when they appear versus rendered emoji.
6. **Sentence and paragraph length** - short and clipped vs long and winding; average
   sentence length; whether thoughts run together or stay atomic.
7. **Message segmentation** - one long message vs several short bursts; where line breaks
   fall; whether the person splits a thought across multiple sends.
8. **Greetings and sign-offs** - how messages open ("hey", "yo", "Hi X,", none) and close
   ("thanks", "cheers", initials, nothing).
9. **Register and formality** - casual vs formal, slang, profanity, hedging ("maybe",
   "i think"), directness, warmth. Note if register shifts by audience.
10. **Recurring expressions** - pet phrases, filler words, catchphrases, characteristic
    transitions ("anyways", "tbh", "to be fair"), and idiosyncratic word choices.

## Confidence note

The corpus may be too thin to characterize a dimension - or the whole voice -
confidently. State confidence honestly:

- If the whole corpus is small (roughly a handful of short messages), open the profile
  with a **low-confidence** caveat: the profile is a best guess and may not generalize.
- For any individual dimension with little or no evidence, mark it **sparse** and say the
  observation is tentative rather than presenting it as an established trait.
- Never inflate a single occurrence into a "habit". Distinguish "seen once" from
  "consistent pattern" in the wording.
